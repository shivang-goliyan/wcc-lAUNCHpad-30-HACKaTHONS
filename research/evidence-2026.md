# Problem evidence — Nami (elder companion, India)

Compiled 4 Oct 2026 for WCC Launchpad 30 (Agentic AI track). Strengthens and verifies `problem-research.md` and `connection-research.md` (section "Elderly parents living alone").

Format: **claim** — number — source (publisher, year, n) — URL — exact quote — confidence.
Confidence: **high** = number read on the primary document or an official release; **medium** = read on a reputable secondary source quoting the primary (news, PIB summary, abstract); **unverified** = could not see the number in any source text, do not put on a slide.

## 1. Scale: elders living alone or only with a spouse

Note on recency: LASI Wave 1 (fieldwork 2017–18, Sikkim 2021) is still the latest national dataset. Wave 2 is in field (field-agency tenders Dec 2024–Apr 2025; IIPS Director Report 2024-25 says "about to be launched") — no Wave 2 results found as of Oct 2026. Use Wave 1 and say "latest national survey".

- **About 1 in 4 Indians aged 60+ live with no child at home (alone, or with spouse/others)** — 5.7% alone + 20.3% "with spouse and/or others" — LASI Wave 1 Elderly Factsheet (IIPS / MoHFW, 2017–18 data, published 2021, n = 31,902 aged 60+) — https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf — Table rows 22–23: "Living alone (%) 5.7 [M 2.6, F 9.0, Rural 6.6, Urban 4.4]"; "Living with spouse and/or others (%) 20.3 [M 26.5, F 14.6]" — confidence: high. (Caveat: "spouse and/or others" can include non-child relatives; say "no child in the household", not "alone".)
- **Elderly women are 3.5× as likely as men to live alone** — 9.0% vs 2.6% — same LASI table, row 22 — confidence: high.
- **UNFPA restates it as "one-fifth live alone or only with the spouse"** — UNFPA + IIPS, *India Ageing Report 2023* (26 Sep 2023, based on LASI) — https://india.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/iar_2023_rgb_web.pdf (p.35) — "While majority of the elderly are still living with their adult children in India, about one-fifth either live alone or only with the spouse and hence, must manage their material and physical needs on their own (Figure 2.15). Around 2.5 percent of the elderly men and 8.6 percent of the elderly women were living alone in India according to the LASI survey." — confidence: high.
- **Over 15% of elderly women in south India live alone** — UNFPA IAR 2023, p.35 — same URL — "Over 15 percent of elderly women in south India were living alone (as compared to 3.9 percent men)" — confidence: high.
- **Newer number (PLFS 2023-24): ~20% of elderly live alone or only with another elderly person; 21.6% urban, 18.3% rural** — analysis by Vidya Mahambare, Poonam Munjal, Palash Baruah (Great Lakes / ThePrint op-ed, 28 Jan 2026, from Periodic Labour Force Survey 2023-24 household rosters) — https://theprint.in/opinion/elderly-care-crisis-budget-2026/2838225/ — "Around 20 per cent of the elderly population lives alone or with another elderly household member." … "There is a slightly higher prevalence in urban areas, at 21.6 per cent, as compared with 18.3 per cent in rural areas." — confidence: medium (op-ed computing from microdata; not an official MoSPI table, but more recent than LASI). The op-ed's "≈30 million" is the authors' extrapolation — unverified as an official figure.
- **The UN agency names migration of children as the cause** — UNFPA IAR 2023, p.35 & p.~120 — same URL — "The disintegration of families … has become a prominent phenomenon as the younger generation is migrating to urban areas for employment" and older persons "are often left behind at their ancestral homes with little or no support as their children migrate to other cities and towns for employment. This has deeply affected the mental health of older persons." — confidence: high (qualitative).
- **"Elderly caring for the elderly"** — UNFPA IAR 2023, ch.8 — same URL — "A distinct reality highlighted through the NSE case study is of the elderly caring for the elderly as the next generation migrates in search of livelihoods." … "In recent years, India witnesses substantial increase in the number of elderly living alone or exclusively living with spouse." — confidence: high (qualitative).

### Projections
- **149 million aged 60+ in 2022 (10.5%) → 347 million (20.8%) by 2050** — UNFPA IAR 2023, p.3 — https://india.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/iar_2023_rgb_web.pdf — "There are 149 million persons aged 60 years and above in 2022 (as on 1 July), comprising around 10.5 percent of the country's population. By 2050, the share of older persons will double to 20.8 percent, with the absolute number at 347 million." — confidence: high.
- **Elderly will outnumber children (0–15) by 2046** — UNFPA India press release, 27 Sep 2023 — https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report — "elderly population will have surpassed the population of children (aged 0 to 15 years)" by 2046 — confidence: medium (read via page summary).
- **80+ population to grow ~279% (2022–2050), mostly widowed, highly dependent women** — UNFPA IAR 2023, p.~37 — "80+ years will grow 279 percent with a predominance of widowed …" — confidence: high.
- **230 million aged 60+ by 2036, ~15% of population, "nearly one in seven Indians"** — PIB backgrounder *Elderly in India: Population, Challenges and Government Initiatives* (Govt of India, 28 Oct 2025; source: MoHFW Technical Group Population Projections 2011–2036) — https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/oct/doc20251028678601.pdf — "the elderly population (60 years and above) projected to more than double from 100 million in 2011 to 230 million by 2036. This evolution indicates that by 2036, nearly one in seven Indians will be aged 60 years or older" — confidence: high.
- **Elderly share 10.1% (2021) → 15% (2036)** — UNFPA IAR 2023, p.21 — "the share of the elderly population is projected to increase from 10.1 percent in 2021 to 15 percent in 2036" — confidence: high.
- **54% of elderly women are widowed** — LASI Wave 1 factsheet, row 7: "Widowed (%) 36.2 [M 16.5, F 54.0]" — confidence: high.

## 2. Children migrate, parents stay behind

- **36% of Indian parents aged 60+ have at least one migrant child; 35% are "empty nesters" (no child in the household)** — Sarkar, Kasemi, Majumder et al., *SSM – Population Health* vol 24, Dec 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.ssmph.2023.101503 (peer-reviewed, LASI 2017-18, n = 19,401 aged 60+) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352827323001684 — abstract: "Results show that 36 percent of older parents have at least one migrant child, and 35 percent are empty nesters. … Our study reveals that empty-nested elderly have a higher prevalence of poor self-rated health and depression. Irrespective of migrant children, the availability of children in the household matters most regarding parents' health care" — confidence: high (abstract read via DOAJ API; upgrades the ⚠ in connection-research.md).
- **The same paper's policy ask is literally Nami's job** — same source — "policies should prioritize raising awareness among migrant children of older adults about the importance of maintaining frequent contact and visiting their aging parents." — confidence: high.
- **Kerala, India's oldest and most migrant state: 1 in 10 households is "senior-only"; ~21% of elders live without any non-aged support** — IIMAD, *Growing Old in Kerala: A Gendered Revisit* (S. Irudaya Rajan et al., Jun 2025, report to Kerala State Planning Board; analysis of Kerala Migration Survey 2023, 20,000 households, 15,716 aged persons) — https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Growing-Old-in-Kerala-A-Gendered-Revisit.pdf — "10.4 per cent of households in Kerala consist of aged persons only … This suggest that one in ten households in Kerala can be classified as 'Senior only household' … About 21 per cent of the total aged are living without non-aged support." Also: "18.4 per cent of households with older persons have no adults aged between 18 and 59 years." and "among older individuals living alone, 76.3 percent are women" — confidence: high.
- **Kerala surveyors had to visit 5,000 extra houses because they were locked — whole families had emigrated, sometimes leaving "only elderly individuals"** — Kerala Migration Survey 2023 report (IIMAD / GIFT, funded by NORKA, n = 20,000 households) — https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf — "migrants obtaining skilled jobs and moving with their families. In such instances, there were fewer family members left behind, sometimes only elderly individuals. … the team had to visit an additional 5000 households due to locked houses (because all members of the families had moved outside India)" — confidence: medium (the PDF itself is stamped "Draft Report not to be quoted"; cite as KMS 2023 and say "draft").
- **Kerala emigrants: 2.2 million (2023)** — KMS 2023, same URL — "According to the KMS 2023, the number of emigrants from Kerala is estimated to be 2.2 million" — confidence: medium (same draft caveat).
- **India has the world's largest diaspora: 18.5 million emigrants in 2024, tripled since 1990** — UN DESA International Migrant Stock 2024, via Data For India (Nileena Suresh, 7 Feb 2025) — https://www.dataforindia.com/international-migration/ — "India has the world's largest emigrant population, with over 18.5 million people of Indian origin living overseas in 2024." … "Between 1990 and 2024, the number of Indian emigrants tripled from 6.5 million to 18.5 million." — confidence: medium (secondary on UN dataset; primary: https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/content/international-migrant-stock).
- **13,35,878 Indian students abroad for higher studies (2024)** — MEA reply in Rajya Sabha, 1 Aug 2024, via Business Standard/PTI — https://www.business-standard.com/external-affairs-defence-security/news/over-1-3-mn-indian-students-pursuing-higher-studies-abroad-in-2024-govt-124080101441_1.html — "13,35,878 Indian students are pursuing higher studies abroad in 2024" — confidence: medium. (MEA's 2025 figure fell to ~12.54 lakh higher-ed per news reports — unverified against MEA PDF.)
- **40.2 crore internal migrants (28.88% of population)** — EAC-PM *400 Million Dreams* (Dec 2024), carried over from connection-research.md — https://www.drishtiias.com/summary-of-important-reports/eac-pm-report-on-domestic-migration-1/print_manually — confidence: medium (not re-verified this round; it is an estimate for 2023 from Railways/telecom data, not a census).

## 3. Medicines and follow-ups: chronic disease, missed doses, missed visits

### Disease burden (60+)
- **36.2% of Indians 60+ have hypertension when actually measured; 21.6% have it undiagnosed** — LASI Wave 1 Elderly Factsheet (IIPS/MoHFW, 2017–18, n = 31,902) — https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf — rows 119–120: "Hypertension (%) 36.2"; "Undiagnosed hypertension (%) 21.6" (footnote: measured systolic ≥140 or diastolic ≥90, not diagnosed by a health professional) — confidence: high.
- **Among those diagnosed with hypertension, only 42.3% are adequately treated** — same factsheet, rows 121–123: "Untreated hypertension (%) 10.0"; "Undertreated hypertension (%) 35.8"; "Adequately treated hypertension (%) 42.3" — confidence: high (denominator is diagnosed hypertensives — say "of those already diagnosed", not "of all elders").
- **Self-reported diagnosed diabetes 14.2% of 60+ (26.1% urban)** — same factsheet, row 55: "Diabetes or high blood sugar (%) 14.2 … Rural 9.3, Urban 26.1" — confidence: high.
- **Nearly half of elders (48.0%) have difficulty with at least one IADL — a list that explicitly includes "making telephone calls" and "taking medications"** — same factsheet, row 109 "Any IADL limitation (%) 48.0 [F 56.7]"; footnote 53: "IADL includes preparing a hot meal, shopping for groceries, making telephone calls, taking medications, doing work around the house or garden, managing money … and getting around or finding address in unfamiliar place" — confidence: high. (Don't claim 48% can't take their medicines — it's "any one or more" of seven tasks.)
- **43.4% of elders who sought outpatient care took medicine without consulting a provider** — same factsheet, row 140 — confidence: high.

### Missed doses
- **Polypharmacy (5–9 medicines) in 49% of Indian adults 60+; hyperpolypharmacy (≥10) 31%; potentially inappropriate medication 28%** — Bhagavathula et al., *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 2021, systematic review & meta-analysis, 27 studies — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8173298/ — "the pooled prevalence of polypharmacy was 49% (95% confidence interval: 42-56 …), hyperpolypharmacy was 31% (21-40 …), and PIM use was 28% (24-32 …) among older Indian adults" — confidence: high. (Mostly hospital/clinic samples — say "studies of older Indian patients".)
- **41.0% of community-dwelling elders on long-term medicines had low adherence; forgetfulness was the #1 reason for missed doses** — Jyotsana, Pandit et al., *Cureus* 2026, community cross-sectional, rural + urban Gujarat, Feb 2023–Jun 2024, n = 380 aged ≥60, MMAS-8 — https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.111631 (PMC13408220) — "Among 380 participants, 194 (51.1%) demonstrated high medication adherence, whereas 156 (41.0%) had low adherence. … Forgetfulness was the most commonly reported reason for missed medication doses." — confidence: high (abstract; single-state sample).
- **48% of Indian hypertension patients don't adhere to their BP medicines (40 studies, 18,808 patients)** — *Current Hypertension Reviews* 2025, SR/MA, PROSPERO CRD42024489527 — https://doi.org/10.2174/0115734021377630250903105143 — "The pooled prevalence of non-adherence to antihypertensive medication was 48% (95% CI 39%-56% …) … A higher prevalence was also observed in community settings (51%) and rural areas (57%)." — confidence: high. CAVEAT: the same meta-regression found non-adherence "linked to younger age" — this is an all-age figure; don't present it as elderly-specific.
- **Only 51% average adherence among ~2,840 mostly older Indians with NCDs (WHO-SAGE wave 2)** — Tolley et al., *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 2023 — https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2023.1183818 — "The average medication adherence rate was 51% across 2,840 patients with one or more NCDs … The strongest factor significantly predicting non-adherence … was multimorbidity" — confidence: high. (Also notes older age was associated with *better* adherence — honest framing: elders try; multimorbidity and complexity defeat them.)
- **Diabetes: pooled 48% non-adherence (MMAS-8 studies, India, 2020–24)** — Basu, Sengupta, Kundu, *Health Science Reports* 2026, SR/MA of 16 studies — https://doi.org/10.1002/hsr2.71845 — "The pooled prevalence of medication non-adherence among MMAS-8 using studies (n = 4) was 48% (95% CI: 18%-79%) … Predictors of poor adherence included older age, … treatment complexity, … polypharmacy, and health-system access barriers." — confidence: medium (very wide CI, only 4 studies in pool).

### Missed follow-ups (the coordination gap)
- **Half of registered hypertension patients never came back for follow-up (Mumbai, 13,184 patients)** — Hegde et al., *Journal of Human Hypertension* 2023 (Mumbai Hypertension Project, 2019–20) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41371-022-00754-1 — "During the 14-month project period, 6752 (51%) patients followed-up … Despite multiple quality-improvement interventions … loss to follow-up remained high … Only with new systems to organize and incentivize patient follow-up will the Indian private sector contribute to achieving national hypertension control goals." — confidence: high (all ages; urban slum private clinics).
- **66% of hypertension follow-up visits were missed at a district hospital before an outreach fix; a human nudge (ASHA home visit) brought 74.9% back** — Bharadwaj et al., *BMJ Open Quality* 2025 (IHCI, Seoni, MP, 2021–22, n = 2,850 registered) — https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjoq-2024-003124 — "Missed visit proportion decreased from 66% (228/345) in quarter four, 2021, to 22% (40/180) in quarter three, 2022. Of the 1438 individuals counselled by ASHA home visits, 74.9% returned for follow-up." — confidence: high (all ages). Strong evidence that *someone following up* is the missing piece.
- **Tech-only adherence fixes fade without human follow-up** — Madabhushi Hari et al., *PLOS Global Public Health* 2026, SR of 29 South Asian studies — https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0006809 — "The findings highlight a clear 'sustainability gap', where short-term technological or educational bursts often fail to maintain long-term adherence without sustained human interaction or professional follow-up." — confidence: high. (Supports Nami's family-escalation design over a plain reminder app.)

## 4. Access friction: phones, literacy, language, needing someone to go with you

- **Only 39.6% of Indians 60+ are literate (24.2% of elderly women); 56.5% never went to school** — LASI Wave 1 Elderly Factsheet (2017–18, n = 31,902) — https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf — row 1 "Literate (%) 39.6 [M 56.6, F 24.2, Rural 30, Urban 62.5]"; row 2 "No schooling (%) 56.5" — confidence: high. → argues for voice, not text/app.
- **Only 41% of urban elders own a smartphone; just 13% use internet or social media; 71% use basic phones** — HelpAge India, *Understanding Intergenerational Dynamics & Perceptions on Ageing* (INBO report, 13 Jun 2025; n = 5,798 across 10 cities, of whom 30% are 60+ — i.e. roughly 1,700 elders, my arithmetic) — https://www.helpageindia.org/news/helpage-india-report-calls-for-strengthening-intergenerational-bonds/ — "only 41% have smartphones" … "Just 13% use the internet or social media" … "71% of elders use basic phones" — confidence: high (official HelpAge release). Note: urban-only sample, so rural is likely worse.
- **81% of urban elders say young family members should accompany them to doctors** — same HelpAge 2025 release — "81% of elders and 82% of youth believe youth should accompany elders to doctors" — confidence: high. → doctor visits are culturally a family task; when the family is in another city, the task falls through.
- **Elders' #1 barrier to talking with the young: "busy schedule" (76%)** — HelpAge 2025 via PARI library summary — https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/helpage-india-report-2025/ — "The busy schedule of youth is seen as the biggest barrier to intergenerational communication by elders (76 per cent) and youth (74 per cent) alike." — confidence: medium.
- **NSS 2025: only ~2 in 5 people aged 60+ can use the internet on any device; more than half still rely wholly or partly on feature phones; only 8% know how to report a cyber fraud** — Sohini Paul & Sudeshna Dey, analysis of NSS Comprehensive Modular Survey: Telecom 2025 (NSO, 80th round, Jan–Mar 2025), ThePrint / Ideas for India, 25 Sep 2026 — https://theprint.in/opinion/from-aadhaar-to-upi-how-indias-digital-rails-can-include-not-exclude-older-adults/3053392/ — "More than half of people aged 60 and above rely exclusively or partly on basic or feature phones, and only around two in five report being able to use the internet through any device." … "Only 8% of people aged 60 and above report knowing how to complain about cybercrime or report fraud through the cybercrime portal" — confidence: medium (official NSO microdata, but the 60+ cut is the authors' analysis; MoSPI's published summary leads with 15–24 and all-adult figures).
- **Only 9.4% of Indians 60+ use the internet (IHDS-3)** — NCAER, *Evolving Landscape of Digital Inclusion in India* (Jun 2026; India Human Development Survey wave 3, 2022–24), via Vision IAS — https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-06-09/miscellaneous/ncaer-report-highlights-hidden-digital-divide-in-india — "only 9.4% of the elderly (60+)" — confidence: medium (secondary; NCAER primary not opened). Different survey definitions explain the gap vs the NSS "2 in 5 able to use".
- **Hindi is the mother tongue of 43.63% of Indians; English the mother tongue of ~2.6 lakh** — Census 2011 language tables (released 2018), via Scroll — https://scroll.in/article/884754/surging-hindi-shrinking-south-indian-languages-nine-charts-that-explain-the-2011-language-census — confidence: medium. → Hindi-first voice is the right default; English-only apps exclude most elders.

## 5. Safety, depression, loneliness

### Depression is mostly invisible
- **8.3% of Indians 60+ screen positive for depression, but only 0.8% have ever been diagnosed — about 1 in 10 cases is known to a doctor** — LASI Wave 1 Elderly Factsheet (2017–18, n = 31,902) — https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf — row 118 "Prevalence of depression based on CIDI-SF (%) 8.3 [F 9.2, Rural 9.2]"; row 65 (self-reported diagnosed) "Depression (%) 0.8" — confidence: high. ("1 in 10" is my ratio of the two printed numbers: 0.8/8.3 ≈ 0.096.)
- **Elders living alone: 13.6% depressed vs 8.4% of those co-residing; widowed + alone = 56% higher odds** — Srivastava et al., *Scientific Reports* 2021 (LASI, n = 30,639 aged 60+) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-01238-x — "13.6% of the older adults who were living alone suffered from depression. Further, 8.4% of the respondents who were co-residing with someone were suffering from depression. … Older adults who were widowed and living alone were 56% more likely to suffer from depression [AOR: 1.56; CI 1.28, 1.91]" — confidence: high.
- **Empty-nest elders: 1.83× odds of major depressive disorder vs those living with all their children** — LASI-based study, as quoted in search snippets ("Older adults living in an empty nest (OR=1.83 95% CI: 1.38–2.42) or were left behind (OR=1.5 …)") — confidence: unverified (could not open the primary paper; use the Sarkar 2023 SSM-PH abstract in §2 instead).

### Loneliness
- **13.4% of Indian elders report frequent loneliness** — Srivastava & Srivastava, *Indian Journal of Psychiatry* 2023 (LASI Wave 1, n = 30,394 aged 60+) — https://doi.org/10.4103/indianjpsychiatry.indianjpsychiatry_594_22 — "13.4% of elderly reported frequent loneliness." — confidence: high.
- **Among 10,000 elders surveyed on "solo ageing": 26.7% live alone because the young family members moved out; 41% say living alone hurt their mental/emotional health; ~31% always or often feel lonely** — Agewell Foundation, *Study on Solo Aging with Special Focus on the Status of Older People Living Alone* (2024, n = 10,000), via Outlook Money/PTI — https://retirement.outlookindia.com/retirement/news/solo-ageing-a-boon-or-bane-agewell-study-uncovers-interesting-facts — "26.7 per cent live alone because the young family members have moved out" … "around 41 per cent of the respondents said it had a negative impact on their mental health" … "Around 10 per cent said they always feel lonely and nearly 21 per cent said they often feel lonely." — confidence: medium (NGO survey, non-probability sample, method not public).

### Falls and being found
- **About 1 in 8 Indian elders fell in the past two years (12.6%); 5.6% had a fall injury; 5.8% fell more than once** — Muhammad, Maurya et al., *Scientific Reports* 2023 (LASI Wave 1, n = 28,285 aged 60+) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-27010-3 — "12.63%, 5.64% and 5.76% older adults reported falls, fall-injury and multiple falls respectively." Barik et al., *BMC Geriatrics* 2022 (n = 28,567) gives 12.5% — https://doi.org/10.1186/s12877-022-03158-5 — confidence: high.
  - ⚠ **Correction to connection-research.md:** it says "22.9% fell in past 2 years". The LASI factsheet does print "Fall (%) 22.9" (row 82), but every peer-reviewed analysis of the same LASI microdata reports ~12.5–12.6% over two years. The 22.9 likely has a different denominator (e.g., among those reporting injury). **Use 12.6% ("1 in 8"), not 22.9%.**
- **Nearly half of elders struggle with at least one instrumental daily task; those with more limitations fall more** — Nagarkar & Kulkarni, *BMC Geriatrics* 2022 (LASI, n = 31,477) — https://doi.org/10.1186/s12877-022-02879-x — "The study reported 6352 fall episodes in 3270 participants aged 60 and above, over a period of 2 years." — confidence: high.
- **Story that makes the gap concrete (Jul 2026): 70-year-old Pune woman lay dead at home for three days after a suspected heart attack; her son lives in Singapore; discovered only because the maid came back** — Punekar News, 12 Jul 2026 — https://www.punekarnews.in/?p=239811 — she "remained alone in her home for three days"; son "settled in Singapore"; "repeated attempts to ring the doorbell went unanswered" … "Police personnel forced open the door and found the woman lying dead inside the house." — confidence: high (as a reported case; anecdote, not a statistic). Similar: Jhansi elderly couple (~70 and 65) believed dead "four to five days" before discovery (Daily Pioneer, 30 Aug 2026, https://dailypioneer.com/news/slug-lite/elderly-couple-bodies-found-home-jhansi-up-probe?year=2026). A daily check-in that escalates when missed is exactly what was absent.

### Healthcare access when alone
- **Rural elders living alone/with spouse (Wardha, 2024, n = 400): 33% cite transport difficulty, 21% long waits, 17% money; 74% have a chronic illness** — K N, Jakasania, Mundra et al., Research Square preprint 2025 — https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7642824/v1 — "barriers persisted: transportation difficulties (33%), financial constraints (17%), and long waiting times (21%) were frequently cited. Nearly three-fourths (74%) reported at least one chronic illness." — confidence: medium (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed).

### Numbers NOT to use
- "18.7% of elderly women and 5.1% of men now living alone" (Sankala Foundation / NITI Aayog *Ageing in India: Challenges and Opportunities*, 2025, as reported by Outlook Money) — conflicts with LASI's own 9.0% / 2.6%; probably a different denominator. **Unverified — don't use.**
- "30% of elderly experience depressive symptoms" (same Sankala report) — symptom scale, not CIDI-SF; inconsistent with 8.3%. Use the LASI factsheet figures.

## 6. The children's side: worry, distance, and "the neighbour" as the emergency plan

Caveat up front: there is no large, recent, independent (government/academic) survey of migrant or NRI children's caregiving in India. What exists is industry-commissioned. Use these as supporting colour, not headline stats — and say so if a judge asks. This gap is itself a point for Nami's own pilot survey.

- **86% of adult children living away worry about their parents' well-being; 65% say their first move in a parent's medical emergency is to call friends and neighbours** — Aditya Birla Health Insurance, *Indian Parental Care Survey* (Oct 2019; n = 2,000 in 10 metro/Tier-1 cities: 998 parents aged 60–70 with at least one child living away + 1,002 children aged 30–45 living far from a parent) — https://bwhealthcareworld.com/article/indian-parents-and-children-have-varied-views-on-health-well-being%E2%80%99-reveals-aditya-birla-health-insurance%E2%80%99s-%E2%80%98indian-parental-care-survey%E2%80%99-178234 (date confirmed via Outlook Money, 8 Oct 2019: https://www.outlookmoney.com/insurance/a-small-percentage-of-senior-citizens-has-health-insurance-cover-3681) — "Almost 86 per cent of Indian children feel concerned about their parent's well-being" … "65 per cent of children living away said that they would first turn to friends and neighbours in a medical emergency. 11 per cent of children said that they would reach to their parents as early as possible. Whereas, more than 50 per cent of parents admit that their children may not necessarily be able to attend to them personally." — confidence: medium (insurer-commissioned, 2019 — older than preferred, but it is the only survey that asks exactly this question with a disclosed n).
- **54% of parents miss their children when going for a routine health check-up** — same survey — "54 per cent of Indian parents still miss their children while going for a routine health check-up. Merely 33 per cent of Indian parents go for routine health check-up once every six months." — confidence: medium.

**Where the gap actually is (stated only from the evidence above):**
1. **Follow-through, not reminders.** Reminder apps exist; evidence says reminders alone fade ("sustainability gap … without sustained human interaction or professional follow-up", PLOS GPH 2026), while a human follow-up nudge brought 74.9% of patients back (BMJ Open Quality 2025). Nami's differentiator must be the loop: missed → escalate → a family member *accepts* → closed.
2. **The elder can't do the admin step.** 48% have an IADL limitation (incl. phone calls, medicines), 39.6% literate, ~41% (urban) own a smartphone, 81% expect a family member to accompany them to the doctor. Phoning the clinic on their behalf (with approval) is the task that falls through when children are away.
3. **The current emergency plan is "call a neighbour"** (65% of children-away, 2019) and the state helpline only hears from elders who dial in.
4. **Paid human desks cost ₹2k–₹11k/month** and cluster in big cities; a voice agent is the cheap default layer beneath them, not a replacement.
- Don't claim "no one does daily check-ins or medicine reminders in Hindi" — Saathi/JunoDost do.

**Problem narrative (3 sentences, built only from the eight above):**
India will have 347 million people over 60 by 2050, and already 35% of older parents have no child living with them and 36% have a child who has migrated; in Kerala, one household in ten is seniors-only. These parents carry illness that mostly isn't managed: over a third have high blood pressure but only 42% of diagnosed cases are adequately treated, 41% of elders on long-term medicines take them poorly (forgetting is the main reason), and depression is about ten times more common than its diagnosis. An app alone won't fix it, because only 41% of urban elders own a smartphone and 81% expect family to take them to the doctor, but follow-up does work: when a health worker followed up, missed visits fell from 66% to 22%, and that follow-up is the job nobody is doing when the children live elsewhere.
